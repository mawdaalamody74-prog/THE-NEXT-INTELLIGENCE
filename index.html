THE DATA

Where does the journey begin?

What is data?

Data is a collection of raw facts, measurements, and observations—numbers, text, clicks, images, or sensor readings—captured before they are interpreted.

Raw data vs. information

Raw data is unprocessed and often unclear on its own. Information emerges when data is organized and given context so it can answer a question.

Everyday and business examples

  • Daily life: step counts, weather readings, and messages.
  • Business: sales receipts, website visits, and customer feedback.

Slide 04

THE INTELLIGENCE

When machines learn from patterns

AI and understanding data

Artificial intelligence uses algorithms to find patterns, relationships, and signals in large or complex datasets that are difficult to analyze manually.

How machine learning learns

Machine learning models improve by learning from examples: they adjust internal parameters to reduce errors and generalize patterns from training data.

A simple example

An email app can learn from examples of messages people mark as spam or not spam, then use those patterns to sort new incoming messages.

Slide 05

THE ANALYSIS

Discovering patterns and anticipating what comes next

Descriptive analysis

Looks at past and current data to understand what happened and what is happening now—summaries, trends, and reports.

Predictive analysis

Uses historical patterns and models to estimate what may happen next, helping teams prepare rather than react late.

A real-world application

A public transport service can review past ridership patterns to understand busy periods (descriptive) and estimate future demand to plan schedules and staffing (predictive).

Slide 06

THE DECISION

Turning insight into better choices

Data-driven insights help people and organizations compare options, reduce uncertainty, and act with clearer evidence.

Important: Predictions support decisions; they do not guarantee what will happen. Context, judgment, and changing conditions still matter.

Business example: e-commerce

An online store can analyze browsing and purchase patterns to decide which products to restock, when to run promotions, and how to improve the shopping experience—while monitoring real outcomes and adjusting.

?

Guess-based decision

  • Relies on assumptions or intuition alone
  • Hard to explain or measure consistently
  • Reactive when results are unexpected
  • Higher risk of overlooked patterns

Clarity: limited

VS

Data-driven decision

  • Grounded in evidence and observed patterns
  • Clearer reasoning and measurable outcomes
  • Supports proactive planning and iteration
  • Combines insight with human judgment

Clarity: stronger

THE FUTURE

Building intelligent systems with responsibility

Important directions

Wider use of AI in everyday tools, better real-time analytics, and more collaboration between humans and intelligent systems across industries.

Human oversight

People remain essential to set goals, interpret context, question results, and ensure systems align with values and real-world needs.

Quality, privacy, responsibility

Trustworthy outcomes depend on high-quality data, respect for privacy, transparency where possible, and responsible use of technology.

The future is not only about smarter machines—it is about wiser choices, guided by evidence and shaped by people.
